# Retrieval and context sufficiency for agent workflows

This is a public scoping review with a search cutoff of **2026-10-08 UTC**, not a systematic or exhaustive review. It covers twenty primary research sources spanning retrieval, long-context use, compression, decomposition, attribution, and changing facts. The source catalog records publication/version status, reviewed evidence, and limitations. No private memory corpus or personal workflow statistics inform the conclusions. “Fulltext” in the catalog means selected method, result, and limitation sections were inspected; it does not mean an experiment was reproduced.

The central synthesis is that a successful search does not establish a complete task context. Ranking, evidence assembly, generation, attribution, and execution are separate stages. Their benchmarks measure different outcomes: BEIR measures retrieval generalization; RULER measures controlled context behaviors; ALCE measures answers and citations. Applying them to an agent requires preserving these distinctions. **Inference:** a retrieved document, a supported answer, and a successfully completed action should therefore have separate evidence records. [RC03](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/65b9eea6e1cc6bb9f0cd2a47751a186f-Abstract-round2.html), [RC08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654v3), [RC17](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)

## Retrieval: complementary signals, conditional strengths

BM25 ranks by weighted term overlap, with term-frequency saturation and document-length normalization. Its theoretical account also makes an instructive assumption: document relevance is assessed independently of other documents. The authors explicitly acknowledge that one document can become useful only in combination with another. **Inference:** literal search is a valuable acquisition route for exact identifiers, error strings, filenames, and domain terminology, provided tokenization preserves the distinguishing string. It is not an evidence-set completeness test; shared terminology can retrieve a wrong version or a document missing a prerequisite. [RC01](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~sbrp622/papers/foundations_bm25_review.pdf)

"Lexical," "sparse," and "semantic" should not be used interchangeably. BM25 is lexical; learned sparse models can add semantic expansion terms; dense models encode query and document meaning in continuous representations. [RC05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02558v2) BEIR compared architectures across eighteen heterogeneous English datasets and found BM25 a robust baseline, while reranking and late interaction often performed well at higher cost. These are historical zero-shot results, not a claim that present dense models are generally inferior. Its neural experiments truncated documents to 512 word pieces, and its benchmark did not comprehensively test long documents, authority, or recency signals. [RC03](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/65b9eea6e1cc6bb9f0cd2a47751a186f-Abstract-round2.html)

Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) combines each document’s reciprocal rank across result lists, avoiding the need to compare arbitrary raw scores. The original study evaluated TREC runs and LETOR rankings; its frequently reused constant, 60, came from a pilot. **Inference:** fusing lexical and semantic candidates is a simple hedge against different misses, not a universal optimal ranker. It cannot recover evidence absent from every candidate list, and consensus among similar rankers can still favor redundant documents. Candidate depth and source diversity matter alongside the fusion rule. [RC02](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~gvcormac/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf)

BRIGHT changes the relevance problem: useful documents may supply a theorem, algorithm, or explanatory principle rather than paraphrase a query. It uses naturally occurring and curated questions across twelve domains, with domain-aware relevance annotation; query reasoning and rewriting can improve retrieval. Its relevance labels remain partly subjective, and ranking helpful documents does not certify a complete executable solution. **Inference:** query reformulation should expose the missing concept while retaining exact constraints and identifiers; a generated explanation is a search hypothesis until the retrieved source verifies it. [RC04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12883v4)

The SIGIR 2026 revision of *Lighting the Way for BRIGHT* adds an important reproducibility qualification. It isolates standard bag-of-words BM25 from query-side BM25 weighting (BM25Q), audits duplicates, empty passages, and missing labels, and tests generalization beyond BRIGHT. BM25Q gains are largely benchmark-specific; simple fusion is not uniformly beneficial when the neural component is already strong. **Inference:** an agent-search comparison must specify preprocessing, query weighting, deduplication, and candidate pools. “Hybrid beats keyword search” is too underspecified to transfer into a filesystem design. [RC05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02558v2)

Semantic similarity also does not entail logical compatibility. NevIR presents paired documents differing in negation and corresponding queries; many tested retrievers failed to rank both members correctly, with cross-encoders stronger than bi-encoders and sparse neural models. This controlled contrast is narrower than ordinary search, but directly tests a distinction that topical relevance can conceal. **Inference:** requirements such as “not approved,” “exclude archived,” and “must not overwrite” deserve explicit constraint checks after retrieval. Neither dense similarity nor literal overlap alone establishes that a candidate respects them. [RC06](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.139/)

## Long context: capacity does not establish utilization

*Lost in the Middle* moves relevant information to different positions while holding the task substantially constant, using multi-document QA and key-value retrieval. The tested models often performed worse when useful evidence was in the middle. Its findings concern particular models, prompts, and tasks; they do not establish a permanent architectural law or a present-model ranking. **Inference:** placing critical constraints prominently is sensible, but cannot replace checking whether the answer actually incorporates them. Reordering should be tested with the same evidence and task rather than credited merely because a prompt became shorter. [RC07](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)

RULER extends simple needle lookup to thirteen configurations involving multiple keys or values, variable tracing, aggregation, and QA with distractors. These controlled tasks reveal incomplete retrieval, distractor sensitivity, and failures to combine information as length grows. The paper explicitly limits the claim: proxy-task correlations with realistic work are unverified, depth-specific reporting and prompt robustness are incomplete, and strong performance at a short baseline is not assumed perfect. **Inference:** use RULER-like checks to isolate failures, then test real tasks; one successful passkey lookup is weak evidence for dependable multi-source reasoning. [RC08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654v3)

NoLiMa, accepted at ICML 2025, removes much of the literal overlap between a question and its planted fact. Answering requires a latent association, so its length sweep distinguishes remembering a matching phrase from finding meaning-related evidence. The revised experiment also tests reasoning prompts and literal-match distractors. Performance degradation persists in these harder settings. The setup is still constructed needle retrieval, not a general agent trial; its proposed attention-based explanation is an interpretation rather than a unique causal diagnosis. **Inference:** include paraphrases, aliases, and misleading literal matches when validating context use. [RC09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167v3)

LongBench v2 supplies a complementary 2025 test: 503 multiple-choice problems across document QA, dialogue history, in-context learning, repository understanding, and structured data, with contexts reported in words rather than tokens. It studies direct answering, reasoning, and retrieval, and includes no-context comparisons. Its English-only sample and uneven task/length distributions constrain comparisons. **Inference:** larger-context evaluation should combine controlled ablations with realistic comprehension tasks and report task strata. A multiple-choice accuracy score does not measure an agent’s file edits, recovery behavior, or preservation of every operational constraint. [RC10](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.183/)

Together these designs isolate position, distractor load, latent association, and task complexity. **Inference:** “effective context” is a property of a model–prompt–task–evidence arrangement. A single maximum token count cannot summarize it, and results from older models should motivate current checks rather than be presented as current performance facts. [RC07](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/), [RC09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167v3), [RC10](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.183/)

## Sufficiency: relevant pieces can leave the answer underdetermined

*Sufficient Context* asks whether the supplied material permits a plausible answer without presupposing a ground-truth answer. Its definition deliberately allows a context to be sufficient yet factually wrong. It distinguishes missing information from failure to use available information, and tests a sufficiency autorater against 115 human-labeled cases. Models sometimes answer incorrectly with sufficient material and answer instead of abstaining when material is insufficient. Its selective-generation experiments improve the correctness of answered cases under abstention/coverage evaluation; the QA-centered evidence does not validate arbitrary action prerequisites. [RC11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037)

**Inference:** sufficiency for an agent should be defined relative to each requested outcome. A source naming a deployment target may answer “which target?” while leaving credentials, authorization, dependencies, or the expected final state unresolved. A useful working distinction is: *relevant* material concerns the task; *sufficient* material closes its information dependencies; *reliable* material meets provenance and freshness needs; *usable* material is actually incorporated. This is an operational extension of QA research, not a theorem supplied by these papers. [RC01](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~sbrp622/papers/foundations_bm25_review.pdf), [RC11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037), [RC18](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/m.derijke/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/wallat-2025-correctness.pdf)

## Compression: optimize retained evidence, not brevity alone

RECOMP learns extractive sentence selection and abstractive synthesis, using downstream language-model or QA performance to select useful compressed context. It can return an empty summary when augmentation adds no value. Importantly, its experiments do not support a universal “less context is better” rule: language modeling and open-domain QA differ in how additional retrieved documents help. Dataset-specific training and end-task objectives limit transfer. **Inference:** benchmark a compressor against both uncompressed evidence and a simple extractive baseline; reduced tokens alone are not proof that all necessary facts survived. [RC12](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/bda88ed2892f5e61c9a9bf215c566913-Abstract-Conference.html)

LLMLingua-2 distills compression labels and trains a bidirectional encoder to retain or discard words while maintaining original order. It offers a reusable task-agnostic compression route and evaluates out-of-domain tasks. Its principal training data came from MeetingBank, so generalization remains an explicit concern. Extracting original tokens avoids inventing new wording, but deleting tokens can still change scope or remove a decisive qualification. **Inference:** “faithful” extractive compression should not be interpreted as a formal guarantee of semantic equivalence or completeness for an unseen task. [RC13](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.57/)

LongLLMLingua instead uses question-aware document and token selection, document reordering, adaptive compression ratios, and subsequence recovery. Its ablations test these components rather than treating compression as a single operation. The authors identify recompression overhead for new questions and risks when query–context relationships are subtle. **Inference:** an archive-wide reusable summary and a task-focused evidence pack are different products. Changing the task can invalidate a previously compressed pack even when the underlying files have not changed. [RC14](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.91/)

**Inference:** preserve exact source spans for dates, identifiers, exceptions, negations, and dependent clauses; use summaries to navigate and prioritize, with a route back to original material. This follows from deletion-based compression and the demonstrated difficulty of negation-sensitive retrieval. The reviewed papers do not establish a universally safe compression ratio, nor do their answer scores certify preservation of every user constraint. [RC13](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.57/), [RC14](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.91/), [RC06](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.139/)

## Decomposition: expose dependencies without laundering intermediate guesses

Decomposed Prompting (DecomP) uses a prompted controller and separate subtask handlers, which may be LLM prompts, recursive decomposition, or symbolic functions such as retrieval. Its experiments cover symbolic tasks and multi-hop QA. This makes failures inspectable at the handler boundary; its error analysis also shows that omitted single-hop results can propagate into incomplete final sets. **Inference:** splitting a retrieval task is useful when each subtask has a clear output and evidence obligation. Delegation itself is not evidence that a subtask result is complete or correct. [RC15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02406)

Least-to-Most first decomposes a problem, then solves the parts in sequence using prior answers. It demonstrates easy-to-hard generalization on selected symbolic and mathematical tasks, while explicitly finding that decomposition prompts do not generalize reliably across domains, sometimes even within one domain. **Inference:** an agent should track which later conclusions depend on each intermediate answer and revisit them if that answer changes. Independent branches can run concurrently; a branch that needs another branch’s verified result is not independent merely because both were assigned together. [RC16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625v3)

**Inference:** complete important context is better conceived as closure over task dependencies than as the union of top-ranked snippets. A bridge fact may have weak similarity to the original request yet be essential to connecting two strong matches. Preserve the evidence chain, unresolved slots, and original constraints through each decomposition step. Neither DecomP nor Least-to-Most proves that a generated decomposition enumerates every prerequisite of an open-ended agent task. [RC15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02406), [RC16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625v3)

## Attribution, changing facts, and abstention

ALCE evaluates retrieved-evidence answer generation along fluency, correctness, and citation quality. It distinguishes whether citations support claims from whether needed support is comprehensively supplied, using entailment-oriented automatic evaluation with human comparisons. **Inference:** attach support to specific material claims, including qualifications, rather than treating a bibliography or document count as groundedness. Citation evaluation also needs human spot checks for compound claims, scope, and source errors; automatic entailment is an estimator of support, not independent truth verification. [RC17](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)

The ICTIR 2025 *Correctness is not Faithfulness* paper distinguishes support from causal source use. Its Command-R+ experiment plants short answer phrases in other documents and observes changed citations. This probes post-rationalization; it is not a population estimate for all models. The authors explicitly acknowledge that insertion might change internal generation processes, complicating the causal interpretation. **Inference:** source acquisition logs prove acquisition, and support checks prove a claim–source relationship; neither alone proves that the source caused the reasoning or that a resulting action succeeded. [RC18](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/m.derijke/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/wallat-2025-correctness.pdf)

CRAG broadens factual QA to changing facts, long-tail entities, set operations, comparisons, and false premises, with web and knowledge-graph mock APIs. Its benchmark tasks do not directly test first-stage candidate-pool construction; commercial-system comparisons also use a different live-query protocol from the fixed benchmark. **Inference:** retain the question’s time, source publication/update time, and event or effective time. A semantically relevant record from the wrong period can answer a different question. Benchmark protocol and time alignment matter before comparing reported accuracy. [RC19](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1435d2d0fca85a84d83ddcb754f58c29-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)

FreshLLMs introduces FreshQA and search-augmented FreshPrompt for changing knowledge and false premises, with evaluation modes that separate answer correctness from added hallucinations. It finds that evidence quantity and ordering matter, but its one-query Google Search setup and regularly updated answers limit generalization and reproducibility over time. **Inference:** conflicting timestamps should trigger explicit resolution of scope, version, and provenance—not silent blending. “Newest” is not automatically correct for a historical request. If the decisive conflict remains unresolved, preserve it in the answer or withhold the affected conclusion. [RC20](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.813/)

## What the evidence warrants—and leaves open

The strongest evidence here concerns bounded, public tasks with controlled inputs or explicit annotations. A replication audit supports implementation-level cautions; a synthetic benchmark supports a behavioral failure diagnosis; realistic QA or repository questions improve task resemblance without becoming action-success tests. **Inference:** transfer these methods as diagnostic components, and validate the assembled workflow against concrete outcomes. No reviewed benchmark alone measures all important context for persistent, open-ended filesystem agents. [RC05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02558v2), [RC08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654v3), [RC10](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.183/)

The unresolved question is how to know that an undiscovered dependency is absent. A high rank, more tokens, an elegant summary, or a plausible decomposition does not supply that certificate. **Inference:** report what evidence closes each known dependency and what remains unresolved; acquire more evidence where the gap is specific, and abstain or qualify where it remains decisive. This is a proposed engineering discipline derived from the literature, not a demonstrated universal stopping rule. [RC02](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~gvcormac/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf), [RC12](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/bda88ed2892f5e61c9a9bf215c566913-Abstract-Conference.html), [RC16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10625v3), [RC20](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.813/)
