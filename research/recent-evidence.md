# Recent evidence: restoring context and maintaining state

This supplement reviews 16 additional public primary sources available by **2026-10-08 UTC**. It preserves a broader set of relevant evidence alongside the main agent-memory review. It is a structured scoping review of a specified source set, with selective inspection of abstracts, methods, evaluation protocols, and limitations. It is neither an exhaustive search nor a new public replication. The [source catalog](../sources/recent-evidence.json) records inspected versions, publication status, supplementary primary links, and actual review depth. No benchmark was rerun, and no private material informed this text. Performance rankings and numerical scores are deliberately omitted because heterogeneous protocols do not support a combined leaderboard.

The practical question is how a new agent can recover enough accurate, important context to continue work. This review treats complete evidence retention, useful retrieval, correct current state, and successful continuation as distinct requirements. The design proposals below are inferences for this project, rather than outcomes established by these papers.

## Maintaining state requires more than accumulating memories

**MemoryAgentBench** evaluates incremental ingestion through accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, and selective forgetting. Its FactConsolidation task orders contradictory facts and instructs agents to prefer later information. Tested approaches struggle across the combined competencies. This is evidence about instructed conflict resolution, not proof of physical deletion or real organizational authority handling. ICLR 2026 acceptance is confirmed by the author repository. [RE01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257v4)

**Hindsight** separates world facts, experiences, synthesized observations, and opinions, with retain, recall, and reflect operations. Its retrieval combines semantic, lexical, graph, and temporal paths. The vendor–academic preprint evaluates conversational QA; an ACL 2026 system-demonstration version confirms the implemented architecture. These results do not establish calibrated opinion confidence or complete preservation of every important source detail. [RE04](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12818v1)

**DSV-Mem**, released as an October 6 preprint, targets evolving visual artifacts and textual updates in constructed professional workflows. It probes current and historical state, derivation, changes, and conflict rejection. Governing updates are a major tested difficulty; a provenance-aware ledger is explored. Scenarios are researcher-generated, and benchmark data and evaluation code **will be released**, rather than being verified as already public. [RE06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08102v1)

**Design inference.** A handoff should expose an accepted current-state view together with the evidence needed to reconstruct it. Each governing change should record its source, effective time, authority, affected object, and relationship to prior state. “Recorded later” should remain distinct from “supersedes earlier”: an imported old document, a draft revision, and an authoritative correction require different treatment. Unresolved conflicts should remain visible. A summary that silently chooses one value can make onboarding fast while leaving the next agent unable to justify that choice.

This proposal also separates forgetting from irretrievability. Updating the operational view can stop an obsolete instruction from governing work while retaining its historical explanation. Actual deletion, retention expiry, and authorized removal need their own policy and verification. A benchmark question answered with the new value establishes only one part of that behavior. The project should test historical queries and current-state queries separately, so fixing stale answers does not erase the ability to explain a change.

## Retrieval can evolve as the task becomes clearer

**ReMemR1** augments sequential memory updates with callback queries over historical memory states, trained using outcome and intermediate rewards. It addresses evidence whose relevance becomes apparent only later. The ICLR 2026 author repository confirms acceptance. Crucially, callbacks search generated intermediate summaries rather than original documents: recovering an earlier summary cannot recover details never represented there. [RE02](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23040v5)

**MemCoT** couples iterative search with semantic working state and an episodic search trajectory. Fine-grained evidence localization is followed by surrounding-context expansion; a judge identifies remaining gaps and requests another search. Conversational QA and component analyses support the approach in tested settings. Window and search-depth sensitivity caution against assuming that unlimited expansion or additional iterations always improve answers. [RE05](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08216v3)

**AgenticRAG**, a Microsoft preprint, gives a reasoning model search, find, open, and summarize tools over an enterprise search backend. Benchmark ablations support iterative exploration, while pre-production observations motivate harness choices. The evidence covers retrieval and QA; it does not establish reliable operation for every enterprise corpus, permission model, or deployment workload. [RE08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05538v1)

**Anthropic’s Contextual Retrieval** prepends generated, document-aware explanations to chunks before dense and BM25 indexing, optionally followed by reranking. Its vendor experiments measure retrieval failures across several domains. Outcomes depend on chunking, embedding models, contextualizer prompts, and retrieval depth. Improved chunk recall is not itself proof of correct final answers or faithful generated annotations. [RE14](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval)

**Design inference.** Navigation aids should make original evidence easier to reach. A compact onboarding entry can identify active goals, accepted constraints, open decisions, and likely evidence locations, then let the agent inspect full artifacts as new dependencies emerge. Generated descriptions, extracted facts, and search trajectories should carry clear labels and source links. Failed searches are useful working context, but should not become permanent claims that evidence does not exist. A fresh agent may have a different query or a newly available source.

## Context capacity and context usefulness vary by task

**Retrieval Quality at Context Limit** finds robust positional retrieval for simple factoid questions in Gemini 2.5 Flash near its input limit. This Google-author preprint is a useful counterexample to universal claims of middle-position failure. Its narrow experiment excludes paraphrased or ambiguous questions, competing facts, mixed modalities, and substantive multi-hop reasoning. [RE10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05850v1)

**Chroma’s Context Rot** varies semantic matching, distractors, structure, and input length, alongside selected LongMemEval and repeated-word experiments. It reports task-dependent degradation and provides a public replication toolkit. This vendor technical report challenges extrapolation from easy needle retrieval; its chosen tasks, model snapshots, judge, and limited topic coverage do not characterize every practical long-context workflow. [RE13](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)

**MUDDLE** compares source-only documents with topical hard negatives and approximately length-matched random distractors. Effects differ across tested models. The distractor sweep is markdown-only; image and PDF conditions cover source-only inputs. Residual length differences and single runs limit causal precision. Its COLM 2026 workshop acceptance is explicitly non-archival, rather than a main-conference publication. [RE03](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29477v1)

**Stronger Baselines for RAG with Long-Context LMs**, published at EMNLP 2025, tests DOS RAG: retrieve original passages and restore their document order. Matched inference budgets make this simple baseline competitive with more elaborate pipelines. The study concerns single-document reading comprehension with specified readers and embeddings; preprocessing and embedding costs were not measured end to end. [RE12](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1656/)

**Design inference.** The project’s preference for accuracy and completeness permits generous evidence access. That preference does not require loading every retained artifact into the initial prompt. Test both a comprehensive evidence package and staged inspection, with the same important facts available to each. Measure omissions, irrelevant interference, corrections, and continuation quality. Only then choose the default presentation. A directory hierarchy, a long context window, or an elaborate graph should earn its role through those tests.

## Compare representations, budgets, and outcomes explicitly

**Beyond Memory Leaderboards** evaluates scientific context restoration while varying ingestion, raw-text preservation, retrieval budgets, and modality. Rankings change under controls, and sparse–dense hybrids are strong in relevant tested interventions. Limits include approximate budget matching, a fixed external synthesizer, single runs, incomplete question coverage, and possible public-paper contamination. Its own system’s bootstrap and observation loop are not evaluated. [RE07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16848v1)

**Agent Retrieval Bench** asks which repository files are needed next across coding-related tasks, with frozen base commits and natural and counterfactual no-gold cases. It exposes differences between semantic similarity and task utility, including abstention calibration gaps. File-level exposure does not establish useful span localization, correct patches, or end-to-end agent success; repository coverage is uneven. [RE09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24882v1)

**TAGGRAPH** compares graph variants and BM25 using common extracted notes, while treating a raw-input system as an external reference. Extraction and vocabulary choices affect reachability; graph-diffusion rankings vary by setting. Its principal evidence is retrieval ranking, and incremental graph maintenance is not tested. An earlier version was accepted to a COLM 2026 workshop. [RE11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38353v2)

**The TREC 2025 RAG track overview** separates retrieval, fixed-retrieval generation, end-to-end RAG, and relevance judging. It evaluates answer completeness and sentence-level citation support using automatic and human assessments. Human annotation covers limited narratives and prioritized runs, and support checks focus on the first cited segment. This official shared-task report is not an agent-memory maintenance benchmark. [RE15](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec34/papers/Overview_rag.pdf)

**Redis’s conversational-memory report** compares extracted facts with facts plus hybrid retrieval over raw, topic-split conversation excerpts. The combined path improves its tested LongMemEval configuration. Cross-system charts mix measured and published values; cost estimates omit infrastructure. The mutable page is labeled June 2026 but now cites July work, so its present contents cannot be treated as a frozen June snapshot. [RE16](https://redis.github.io/redis-ai-research-public/longmemeval-agent-memory/)

**Evaluation inference.** Use a staged diagnosis before assigning failure to “memory.” Ask whether the source exists, whether ingestion preserved the needed detail, whether search exposed it, whether the reader understood it, whether state resolution applied the right authority, and whether the agent completed the intended continuation. These stages can fail independently. A filesystem project can implement them with ordinary artifacts, manifests, and recorded retrieval traces; a specialized database is optional until demonstrated requirements justify it.

For comparisons, fix the source corpus and version, allowed evidence, reader model, prompts, and query policy. Record both ingestion and retrieval resources, including hidden extraction calls, retries, background work, and preprocessing. Report actual returned evidence volume rather than treating similarly named top-k settings as equivalent. Run a source-preserving lexical or hybrid baseline, a long-context baseline where feasible, and an oracle-evidence condition for reader diagnosis. A graph comparison should expose whether it changes extraction, representation, retrieval, or several together.

Completeness should reflect the work the next agent must do. Define important constraints and dependencies before evaluating a handoff; include corrections, rejected proposals, incomplete operations, and unresolved questions. Ask a fresh agent to continue a bounded public task, then inspect its actions and resulting artifact. Its ability to repeat a summary is a separate outcome. Test missing-evidence cases where abstention or further inspection is the correct next action, including plausible material from the wrong project and outdated but authoritative-looking artifacts.

## What remains uncertain

The reviewed evidence supports several mechanisms worth testing, without settling their combination for this project. Open questions include which onboarding facts should be mandatory, how to detect important details omitted by extraction, how authority resolution should interact with time, and when iterative retrieval is worth its additional work. Multimodal revisions, dynamic repository changes, concurrent writes, and reliable historical reconstruction need dedicated evaluation. A useful research contribution would publish small, inspectable continuation tasks with ground truth and explicit failure attribution, then expand coverage as limitations become visible.

The source statuses matter: conference research, a system demonstration, workshop papers, preprints, an official track report, and vendor experiments carry different evidence. Peer review does not establish deployment transfer, and a publicly available toolkit does not establish independent reproduction. This supplement supplies candidate mechanisms and reasons to question comparisons. The project’s design should remain a testable proposal until its own public continuation evaluations provide evidence.
