# Evidence packing, provenance and memory boundaries

Research and prototype prepared October 13, 2026. This note separates mechanisms supported by primary literature, hypotheses for this study, implemented policies and execution checks. **No benchmark outcome is claimed here.** The prototype uses fictional retained records and the Python standard library. References and access limitations are recorded in [packing-sources.json](packing-sources.json).

## The question changes when retrieval returns passages

The October 11 study exposed a serialization bottleneck: whole authored evidence bundles often could not fit a 4 KB packet, and index cues added both search information and packet bytes. An informative next experiment separates the representation used to find evidence from the representation sent to a reader. Titles, aliases and inbound references can guide search without being repeated inside every evidence payload. The comparison must hold rankings fixed to isolate packing, and report body bytes, wrapper bytes and actual source coverage. A shorter packet alone is not an accuracy result. [Prior report](../study-2026-10-11/REPORT.md)

RECOMP distinguishes selecting source sentences from generating a compressed paraphrase, and optimizes its trained compressors for downstream utility. Its authors also acknowledge that extraction can lose faithfulness when necessary context disappears. Our source-exact spans remove one opportunity to invent a paraphrase, but they still can omit a condition, negation or unresolved alternative. This prototype is an untrained lexical control; it does not reproduce RECOMP's trained compressor or its reported gains. [RECOMP, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04408v1)

Late Chunking addresses a different stage: token representations see a long document before being pooled into shorter indexed chunks. Its benefit depends on informative surrounding context, and the paper reports that surrounding irrelevant text does not help synthetic needle/passkey tasks. This motivates testing short evidence units while preserving surrounding context during indexing. Our lexical windows do not implement late chunking; a future encoder experiment would need a compatible long-context mean-pooling model, frozen token offsets and a separately measured index cost. [Late Chunking, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04701v3)

The counter-hypothesis deserves equal attention. LongRAG groups related documents into larger units to retain contextual integrity and support multi-document answers. Its ablation discussion notes that answer quality need not rise monotonically with retrieval recall. It motivates retaining whole-document and whole-dependency-bundle arms, rather than assuming finer granularity always wins. [LongRAG, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15319v2)

The implemented observation contract is `id`, half-open Python character offsets, source-exact text, a whole-body flag and a SHA-256 digest of the original UTF-8 body. These allow a reviewer to recover each quotation from an unchanged original. They do not make the original true, validate authority or guarantee that an excerpt is sufficient. UTF-8 packet length includes every selected wrapper and separator. Source hashes live in the receipt rather than being silently counted as reader context. If a deployment transmits the receipt too, its bytes must be measured separately.

## Eight independently inspectable policies

All policies accept the same ranking, query, document map and byte budget. They receive no answer key, required evidence IDs, task-family label, expected status or judge feedback. They retain source records unchanged. Unknown ranked IDs, malformed ledgers, invalid budgets and unencodable text fail explicitly. A source may be absent from a packet because of budget; that omission never deletes the retained original.

|Policy|Selection and payload|Hypothesis and foreseeable failure|
|---|---|---|
|`whole_record`|Ranked whole bodies with title, scope, aliases, dates and declared graph/ledger metadata in each wrapper|Rich wrapper context can aid interpretation but costs bytes. This is a new serialization baseline, not a byte-identical rerun of the October 11 baseline.|
|`ranked_body`|Same rank order and whole bodies, compact identity/kind/revision/offset wrappers|Search metadata can remain outside the packet. Reader loses explicit wrapper scope/graph cues even though the original body is intact.|
|`sentence_neighbors`|At most two lexical sentence windows per record, each including the preceding and following sentence; first window across records before second windows|Source-exact local context fits more evidence. Remote qualifications or lexically weak evidence can be omitted.|
|`closure_passages`|The same windows after declared dependency, incorporation and forward/reverse supersession traversal|Linked originals can recover needed evidence beyond ranked seeds. Missing or incorrect edges and a bounded traversal remain ceilings.|
|`original_first`|The same closure/window candidates, with sources stably ordered before derived views|Original evidence deserves scarce space before convenient summaries. Correct views can help readers and this policy may withhold them until too late.|
|`diversity_passages`|Greedy query-token novelty, branch novelty, rank prior and Jaccard redundancy penalty per serialized byte|Repetition may crowd out useful distinct evidence. Branch diversity is a proxy and can introduce irrelevant topics.|
|`coverage_bundles`|Greedy atomic seed plus immediate declared-neighbor window groups, using query-token coverage per incremental serialized byte|Evidence needs may be conjunctive. An oversized bundle is skipped; a wrong graph can join unrelated records.|
|`authority_pairs`|Atomic whole-body connected components of original supersession records; explicit compatible supersession determines priority; inconsistent derived ledgers are gated|Preserving corrections together with the originals makes changes auditable. Large conflict components may not fit; compatible metadata does not resolve semantic truth.|

Depth-two closure adds at most sixteen new records per seed. Coverage groups use depth one and at most eight new neighbors. Cycles terminate. Missing references and cap omissions are exposed in receipts. Sentence segmentation uses punctuation and newline boundaries, rather than a language parser; abbreviations, code, tables and unusual punctuation can be poorly segmented. At most two windows is a declared ceiling. Larger-scale greedy candidate rescoring is quadratic and needs cached features or a lazy priority queue; no speed claim is made for this implementation.

These are coherent bundled policies, not eight atomic causal factors. A future decomposition should separately switch extraction, original priority, closure, diversity and component atomicity while holding candidate spans and wrappers fixed. Comparing `whole_record` with `ranked_body`, for example, changes both serialized cost and interpretation cues.

## Coverage and diversity do not prove completeness

MMR balances query relevance against redundancy in document and passage selection. It motivates an explicit diversity control rather than trusting a top-ranked list to cover distinct needs. Our Jaccard/query-token variant is a transparent experiment, not the original MMR scoring formula or a calibrated semantic similarity model. [Goldstein and Carbonell, 1998](https://aclanthology.org/X98-1025/)

Budget-constrained submodular summarization supplies principled coverage objectives and makes cost constraints explicit. Our implementation borrows the idea of marginal coverage per cost, but does not claim the paper's guarantees. Query-token coverage is a heuristic proxy. An answer requiring three jointly necessary originals has complementarity: supplying only one can have almost no task value. A greedy set-coverage score over words can miss that structure. Atomic declared-neighbor groups test one limited response to this mismatch. [Lin and Bilmes, 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1134/), [Lin and Bilmes, 2011](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1052/)

HippoRAG 2 connects passages to an associative graph and uses Personalized PageRank alongside online LLM selection. It provides evidence that graph retrieval deserves investigation across factual, associative and sense-making workloads. Our traversal is a bounded declared-edge control, without extracted triples, semantic entity linking, PageRank or an online LLM. GraphRAG's community summaries address broad corpus questions; that motivates a separate global-synthesis workload, rather than assuming the same retrieval unit should answer both a narrow correction question and a thematic overview. [HippoRAG 2, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v1), [GraphRAG, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)

A useful additional avenue is inbound referral indexing. RAR augments a document's search representation with text from documents that cite or link to it. This suggests testing incoming reference descriptions as search-only cues, while still delivering the referenced original and keeping the referral's identity separate. A referring summary is a clue about relevance, not proof that its target endorses every assertion in the referral. [Referral-Augmented Retrieval, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15098v1)

Negative evidence requires its own task class. A failure to retrieve approval does not prove that approval never occurred. An explicit original saying a prerequisite is absent differs from a missing linked file and from a truncated packet. A bounded traversal receipt proves only what was searched under its declared limits. *Sufficient Context* separates inadequate context from failure to use adequate context. The 2026 parallel-world search benchmark also analyzes collection, sufficiency judgments and stopping decisions as distinct bottlenecks. These support separately measuring source-span sufficiency, answer support, abstention and successful artifact production. Neither paper certifies this local filing workflow. [Sufficient Context, 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06037v1), [Evaluating the Search Agent in a Parallel World, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04751)

## Retention, projections and authority are different operations

Event sourcing retains immutable events and builds query-specific materialized views; compensating events can correct a previous event without rewriting it. This is a useful software analogy for preserving session evidence and rebuilding topic views. It is not a recommendation to introduce a distributed event platform for a small vault. Its projection-lag and schema-evolution costs remain relevant even when implemented as files. [Microsoft's event-sourcing pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/event-sourcing)

W3C PROV distinguishes entities, derivation, revision and quotation. A selected passage can therefore have an auditable relation to its retained source, and a summary can have a versioned derivation ledger. However, a ledger that names the latest originals can still accompany an incorrect summary. The prototype's executable check explicitly demonstrates that a false body with a consistent ledger receives `declared_current`. That label is metadata consistency, not endorsement. [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)

Bitemporal databases distinguish when a fact is valid from when the system learned it. This directly cautions against treating a late import as the newest instruction. Future tests should independently vary authored time, import time, effective interval and query-as-of time. The current alternate uses only declared supersession plus literal scope/time-scope equality or an explicit `all` wildcard. It does not infer date intervals from prose. If one record has a `time_scope` and the other omits it, they are not silently declared compatible. Incompatible originals are still retained together in the authority component. [XTDB's valid/system-time documentation](https://docs.xtdb.com/about/time-in-xtdb.html)

The authority policy keeps the entire connected original supersession component as one whole-body packing decision, including differing scopes. A failed budget test exposes the omitted group. This conservatism may perform poorly at small budgets; its purpose is to test a retention-versus-packet tradeoff without silently presenting a correction alone as the complete story. Undeclared contradictions are not detected, and a metadata cycle produces deterministic presentation order rather than an invented authority conclusion.

## Human memory supplies hypotheses, not filesystem prescriptions

In a 2025 intracranial study, ten epilepsy patients watched a continuous narrative. Hippocampal ripple activity increased at event boundaries, while temporal-cortex ripple occurrence within events differed for later remembered versus forgotten events. This is a specific observational human result, with a clinical sample and recording constraints. It does not demonstrate that an agent should use one file per conversation or consolidate at a fixed interval. The software hypothesis is narrower: topic/event boundaries may be useful indexing units, while links retain relationships across those units. Test event/topic segmentation against session-wide capture with the same original content and capture coverage. [Silva and colleagues, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12216404/)

Tversky's 1973 experiments reported different encoding benefits for anticipated recognition versus recall tests: within-item detail and between-item relationships were useful in different ways. The publisher abstract is the accessed evidence here; the full article was not read. This suggests a testable engineering analogy: store both discriminating local identifiers and cross-record relationships, and vary retrieval workload instead of imposing one universal taxonomy. It does not establish a biological optimum for PARA or a direct transfer of human effect sizes. [Tversky, 1973](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0010028573900376)

Long-context placement offers another controlled intervention. *Lost in the Middle* reports position-sensitive performance in its retrieval/QA experiments, motivating fixed-packet order permutations while keeping text, byte cost and supplied evidence unchanged. That cleanly tests reader utilization independently of retrieval. Whether current readers reproduce those effects must be measured, rather than assumed from an older model study. [Liu and colleagues, 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03172v2)

## What to freeze and measure next

The implementation's self-check passes for exact offsets, Unicode/UTF-8 serialization, exact-fit and one-byte-too-small budgets, wrapper and separator costs, immutability, cycle termination, missing references, traversal caps, scope/time-scope mismatch, duplicate or missing ledgers and revision invalidation. These are execution checks on the packer, not evidence of retrieval quality.

Before new outcomes are inspected, freeze candidate construction, ranking inputs, method names, budgets, evaluation spans and held-out allocation. Grade recovery using actual observed spans; merely selecting a required record's ID is an invalid completeness metric after extraction. Retain record-level recall as a separate descriptive measure. Include explicit negation, distant qualifiers, scope exceptions, tables/code, Unicode, conflicting originals, late imports, missing links and falsely current views in independently authored cases. Keep reader success, source support, abstention, discovery and artifact writing distinct.

Further avenues are adaptive span expansion after a support check, source-linked summaries with compulsory original fallback, inbound-referral search cues, valid/system-time interval queries, independently permuted packet order, graph repair from explicit source identifiers, branch-diversity ablations and a global-synthesis workload. All remain proposals in this note. Useful claims should survive a fresh bank and source-grounded answer review before influencing the production vault.

Run the executable checks with `python packing_lab.py --self-check`; import `pack(docs, query, ranked_ids, budget, method)` for frozen benchmark execution.
